## 3. Reading the result

**The scalability claim, made precise (feasibility, not just speed-up).** Exact Shapley evaluates all 2ⁿ-1 coalitions, so its wall-clock grows geometrically — 140s already at n=12 — and becomes flatly infeasible beyond it (n=20 ⇒ 2ⁿ = 1,048,576 coalitions, hours-to-days). The Monte-Carlo permutation estimator costs O(samples·n) and runs every size up to n=20 in seconds-to-minutes. The honest framing is a **crossover**, not a flat speed-up: below n≈10 the faithful (untruncated) MC samples almost all coalitions anyway, so it matches exact cost (speed-up ≈1×) — and that is fine, because exact is cheap there. Above the crossover MC touches a small fraction of 2ⁿ, giving a measured ~4.1× at n=12 and, more importantly, being the *only* estimator that still terminates. **The point is feasibility at scale, not a large constant-factor win.**

**Reward fidelity — honest reading.** Because the orgs have a genuine quality gradient, true Shapley values are non-uniform and the MC weights track them: mean Spearman rank-correlation ρ≈+0.79 vs exact (up to ρ=+1.00), worst-case L1 weight error 0.255, L∞ within ~0.1. The single top contributor is recovered in 5/10 cases — exact top-1 agreement is the *hardest* fidelity bar (it flips on sub-noise weight differences when two orgs are near-tied), so ρ and L1 are the fairer measures of whether the on-chain token split (federation_pool × Shapley weight) is preserved.

**Fidelity vs n — the sample budget must scale.** Fidelity is strong at low–moderate n but degrades at the largest exact n at a *fixed* `shapley_mc_samples`=200: ρ falls to +0.54 at n=6, where 200 permutations under-resolve 6 near-equal contributors (each true weight ≈0.17). This is expected for permutation sampling — to hold fidelity as the federation grows, samples should scale with n (≈O(n log n)); the speed-up figures above are therefore a *lower bound* on the achievable cost at matched fidelity.

**Truncation knob (further speed-up, fidelity cost).** TMC-Shapley (Ghorbani & Zou 2019) early-stops each permutation once the running coalition value saturates, cutting cost to ≈n coalitions total — a large further speed-up, but it discards the higher-order marginals and degrades fidelity (in a smoke test on near-identical orgs it collapsed to singleton marginals). It is left OFF here to keep fidelity maximal and exposed as the config flag `shapley_mc_truncation`.

**The data-dilution confound, controlled.** Two accuracy curves are reported. *Equal-shard* holds each org's data volume fixed as n grows (83.1%→84.7% bal-acc), isolating the effect of more orgs; *fixed-pool* splits one dataset n ways so each org's data — and its few fraud positives — shrink, which on a heavily imbalanced set drives a sharp drop as n grows. The gap is the dilution effect (a data-budget limitation of BankSim (594,643 tx, 1.21 % fraud, 4,112 customer IDs)), NOT a failure of the attribution scaling, and is disclosed rather than hidden.

**Honest scope.** This is *algorithmic* scalability of contribution attribution on a single machine. It does not measure blockchain throughput/latency (leader_block.py is simulated) nor distributed speed-up (orgs run sequentially). Krum still runs at f=0. The defensible title phrasing is therefore 'scalable contribution attribution', not 'scalable ML' unqualified — see title_issue.md §3.
