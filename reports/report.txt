Abstract
The expansion of Machine Learning (ML) is driving innovation in healthcare, fi-
nance, and IoT, but traditional centralized approaches face security, privacy, and
trust challenges. Federated Learning (FL) goes for a decentralized approach but is
struggling with validation overhead, adversarial threats, and trust in model updates.
Here, the study is integrating blockchain with FL and Reinforcement Learning to
strengthen security and data integrity. The Blockchain-Integrated Federated Learn-
ing (B-FL) framework also leverages blockchain’s immutable ledger, Byzantine Fault
Tolerance (BFT), proof of Deep Learning (PoDL), and smart contracts. Moreover,
privacy enhancing techniques such as Fragmented Federated Learning (FFL) and
Additive Homomorphic Encryption (AHE) mitigating attacks such as model poison-
ing attacks and at the same time ensuring data aggregation. Nonetheless blockchain
integration may introduce computational overhead, this study also optimizes con-
sensus mechanisms and incentive structures to create a scalable, privacy- preserving
ML framework applicable to healthcare, finance and IoT



Keywords: Blockchain, Machine Learning (ML), Federated Learning (FL), Rein-
forcement Learning (RL), Blockchain-Integrated Federated Learning (B-FL), Secu-
rity, Privacy, Data Integrity, Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT), Proof of Deep Learn-
ing (PoDL), Smart Contracts, Fragmented Federated Learning (FFL), Additive Ho-
momorphic Encryption (AHE), Model Poisoning Attacks, Consensus Mechanisms,
Incentive Structures, Scalability, Decentralization, Healthcare, Finance, Internet of
Things (IoT)

Chapter 1

Introduction

1.1      Background
Rapid development of Machine Learning has revolutionized industries including
healthcare, finance, and smart cities, creating new opportunities for productivity
and innovation. However, traditional centralized machine learning frameworks have
significant problems with data security, privacy, and dependability [17]. Federated
Learning (FL) has become a viable decentralized method in response, allowing model
training on dispersed devices while maintaining data privacy. Despite its potential,
FL still faces obstacles such the requirement for efficient incentive systems, validation
costs, robustness to adversarial assaults, and trust in model updates [17]. Because
blockchain technology is decentralized and irreversible, researchers are now investi-
gating how it might be integrated with FL to improve security, transparency, and
data integrity [10]. Besides FL, blockchain has been used in Reinforcement Learning
(RL), where Blockchain-enabled Federated Reinforcement Learning (B-FRL) allows
multiple agents to learn together while being transparent and trusted. But there
is a trade-off between security and performance. The field has advanced further
with recent work on data heterogeneity and poisoning attacks, such as Fragmented
Federated Learning (FFL) and privacy-preserving FL models that use Gaussian Mix-
ture Models (GMM) and Additive Homomorphic Encryption (AHE). These show
how blockchain can change machine learning, but we still have communication over-
head, collusion attacks and computational cost. As blockchain and machine learning
continue to develop together, they have the potential to influence the direction of
decentralised intelligence in a variety of fields, including healthcare, finance, the In-
ternet of Things, and more.



1.2      Rational of the Study
Even though the fast evolution of Machine Learning (ML) caused immense amounts
of progress in various fields, we still know that the conventional frameworks of
Machine Learning depend on centralized data processing which lead to situations
where these frameworks have to face challenges related to data privacy, security, and
trustworthiness. Federated Learning (FL) plays a major role in solving this problem
as it enables decentralized model training while at the same time preserving data
confidentiality. Nonetheless, there still remains struggles as Federated Learning (FL)

Blockchain technology is a solution to enhance trust and security within FL systems
by introducing the decentralized, immutable, and transparent ledger mechanisms.
The integration of Federated Learning with Blockchain shows us that it has a vivid
potential to mitigate threats like model poisoning attacks, unauthorised data ma-
nipulation, and privacy threats. Although promising, there are still challenges like
trade-offs in computational efficiency and scalability issues, necessitating for better

The Research goes on addressing the challenges through developing and evaluating a
Blockchain-integrated Federated Learning Framework that improves the accuracy of

connecting the gaps between the Federated Learning and Blockchain, this research
also plays a vital role in the enhancement of secure and decentralized Machine
Learning models, which are significant for applications in healthcare, finance, and
IoT-driven smart environments.



1.3      Problem Statement
The rapid advancement of machine learning (ML) has led to breakthroughs across
various domains, but traditional ML frameworks rely on centralized data storage
and processing. This dependence raises serious concerns regarding data privacy,
security, and trustworthiness. Federated Learning (FL) addresses some of these
issues by enabling decentralized model training while preserving data confidentiality.
However, FL still faces significant challenges, including validation overhead, trust in
model updates, adversarial attacks, and inefficiencies in incentive mechanisms.
Blockchain technology, with its decentralized and immutable ledger, offers a promis-
ing solution by enhancing security, transparency, and integrity in FL systems. It
can mitigate risks such as model poisoning attacks, unauthorized data manipula-
tion, and privacy threats. Despite these advantages, integrating blockchain into FL
introduces trade-offs in computational efficiency, scalability, and system complexity.
Current research lacks an optimized framework that effectively balances security,
performance, and privacy while ensuring practical deployment.
This study aims to develop a Blockchain-Integrated Federated Learning (B-FL)
Framework that enhances security, minimizes validation overhead, and preserves
participant privacy. By leveraging blockchain’s tamper-proof ledger and smart con-
tracts, along with lightweight consensus mechanisms such as Byzantine Fault Toler-
ance (BFT) and Proof of Deep Learning (PoDL), the framework seeks to optimize
operational efficiency. Additionally, privacy-preserving techniques like Fragmented
Federated Learning (FFL) and Additive Homomorphic Encryption (AHE) will be
integrated to defend against poisoning attacks.
The proposed framework will be evaluated based on security, scalability, and model
accuracy. Addressing these challenges will contribute to the advancement of secure,
decentralized ML models applicable to critical areas such as healthcare, finance, and
IoT-driven smart environments.

Literature Review

2.1      Preliminaries
The thesis examines how blockchain can be integrated with Machine Learning (ML)
to strengthen security, privacy, and scalability in Machine Learning based systems.
By reinforcing data integrity and enabling trusted collaboration, Blockchain en-
hances Reinforcement Learning(RL) and Federated Learning (FL), tackling issues
such as inefficiency and trust. Privacy-focused techniques such as FFL and Addi-
tive Homomorphic Encryption (AHE) are incorporated despite their computational
cost. The resulting framework enables secure and scalable Machine Learning solu-
tions with incentives, benefiting industries such as healthcare, finance, and IoT.



2.2      Review of Existing Research
The convergence of blockchain technology and machine learning (ML) has gained
significant attention in recent research, offering novel solutions to critical issues such
as security, privacy, and scalability. Traditional ML frameworks, often centralized,
remain susceptible to data breaches, adversarial attacks, and single points of fail-
ure [15]. Recognizing these vulnerabilities, researchers have explored blockchain’s
decentralized architecture to establish a more secure and transparent ecosystem for
data sharing and model training. Various studies have proposed frameworks that
integrate blockchain with ML, seeking to optimize their interplay while addressing
inherent limitations. In this context, Federated Learning (FL) has emerged as a
prominent decentralized ML technique, allowing models to be trained across dis-
tributed edge devices while preserving data privacy.
Despite its advantages, FL is not without its challenges, particularly concerning
trust, data integrity, and susceptibility to model poisoning attacks [20]. To miti-
gate these risks, blockchain has been positioned as a security-enhancing layer in FL,
leveraging its immutable ledger to verify global model updates and detect malicious
alterations [24]. Researchers emphasize the role of smart contracts and consensus
mechanisms in fostering trust among participating nodes [18], ensuring that only
legitimate contributions are integrated into the training process. While such ad-
vancements bolster the security of FL, concerns remain regarding node selection in-
efficiencies and workload distribution [16]. Blockchain-based incentive mechanisms,
such as Proof of Useful Work (PoUW) [10]. Blockchain-based incentive mecha-

                                            5
nisms, such as token-based bidding systems, have been introduced to optimize these
processes by rewarding reliable and resource-efficient nodes [22]. Beyond FL, Rein-
forcement Learning (RL) presents another avenue where blockchain’s decentralized
nature could mitigate computational bottlenecks and security concerns. Conven-
tional RL frameworks rely heavily on centralized training, limiting their scalability
and exposing them to data integrity risks.
Blockchain-enabled Federated Reinforcement Learning (B-FRL) has been proposed
as a solution, enabling multiple agents to learn collaboratively while ensuring data
security [12]. By utilizing an immutable ledger, B-FRL verifies updates from differ-
ent agents, reducing the likelihood of model corruption. However, the integration of
blockchain in RL introduces a trade-off between computational efficiency and secu-
rity, necessitating careful optimization to maintain practical feasibility [8]. Consen-
sus mechanisms play a pivotal role in determining the effectiveness of blockchain-
integrated ML frameworks. While traditional mechanisms such as Proof of Work
(PoW) are computationally intensive and unsuitable for ML applications, alternative
approaches like Proof of Deep Learning (PoDL) [12] and Byzantine Fault Tolerance
(BFT)-based protocols have been proposed to enhance efficiency. These mecha-
nisms not only uphold network integrity but also incentivize honest participation by
rewarding high-quality data and model contributions. Parallel to this, researchers
highlight the significance of incentive structures in blockchain-ML systems, partic-
ularly for data providers and model contributors. Token-based reward models and
smart contract-based compensation systems have been explored as viable strategies
to ensure a steady supply of high-quality datasets while safeguarding user privacy
and ownership rights.
Expanding on blockchain’s utility, researchers have introduced innovative frame-
works tailored to specific security and scalability challenges in decentralized envi-
ronments [7]. One such advancement is the development of a scalable blockchain
framework designed to enhance the security of Internet of Things (IoT) transac-
tions. [11] This framework employs a dynamic application block generation (DABG)
scheme, coupled with group signature-based authentication and time-bound crypto-
graphic keys, to manage transactions efficiently while ensuring privacy and traceabil-
ity. Such a model addresses the growing need for secure IoT interactions, reinforcing
blockchain’s versatility beyond ML applications. Further integrating blockchain into
FL, researchers have proposed a novel reputation-based miner selection mechanism
to improve Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) attack detection [17]. By lever-
aging a blockchain-backed federated learning approach, this framework utilizes a
random forest model to achieve a 99.1 percent detection accuracy, demonstrating
its efficacy in safeguarding decentralized systems against poisoning attacks. These
findings illustrate the potential of blockchain-integrated ML frameworks to fortify
security in various applications, from AI-driven ecosystems to IoT infrastructures.
Amid these advancements, the concept of Fragmented Federated Learning (FFL)
has been introduced to enhance privacy and security in FL systems [15]. By frag-
menting local model updates and mixing them before transmission. FFL mitigates
the risk of poisoning attacks while preventing semi-honest servers from extracting
private data. This approach ensures a balance between model accuracy and privacy
preservation, facilitating robust global model convergence. However, the additional
communication overhead introduced by FFL presents a challenge, and the method
remains vulnerable to collusion attacks involving multiple malicious participants.

There’s a need to reduce communication costs without hurting model accuracy, es-
pecially against collusion [14].
Future research efforts are directed towards addressing these limitations, improving
scalability, and fortifying defenses against sophisticated adversarial strategies .Com-
plementing these efforts, a privacy-preserving federated learning (PPFL) model has
been proposed to counter model poisoning attacks and address data heterogeneity
in decentralized systems. By integrating Additive Homomorphic Encryption (AHE)
for secure gradient aggregation and employing Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM)
alongside Mahalanobis Distance (MD) for internal auditing, this framework detects
maliciously encrypted gradients across both IID and non-IID data settings[21]. Un-
like existing solutions such as ShieldFL, which struggles with encrypted non-IID
gradients, or TrustFL and SafeFL, which introduce scalability and computational
burdens, this model enhances cryptographic efficiency while maintaining robust se-
curity. However, the reliance on AHE introduces computational overhead for large-
scale deployments, and collusion attacks remain an open challenge. Researchers
suggest future enhancements, including quantum-resistant cryptographic techniques
and blockchain integration, to further improve transparency and security [21].
Recent studies have examined combining Federated Learning with blockchain-based
crowdfunding to improve data security and build trust [3]. FL enables decentralized
model training on local data, safeguarding privacy while generating valuable in-
sights for decision-making. Meanwhile, blockchain secures transactions and funding
records with immutable ledgers and smart contracts, promoting transparency and
integrity. However, issues like system interoperability, transaction throughput, and
energy efficiency persist. Future work should focus on refining these integrations
and addressing scalability challenges for progress [13].
The challenge of computational efficiency in Federated Learning is addressed by
The LVFL, which optimizes Vertical Federated Learning (VFL) by reducing com-
putational and communication overhead through dual lightweighting ratios [19]. It
employs structured and unstructured pruning to enhance efficiency while maintain-
ing convergence. However, its evaluation is limited to the CIFAR-10 dataset, lacks
real-world deployment considerations, and assumes uniform client ratios. Research
gaps include optimizing pruning strategies for diverse datasets, addressing network
latency issues, and improving parameter tuning for heterogeneous environments.
Future work should focus on enhancing adaptability and testing LVFL on complex,
high-dimensional data for broader applicability.
Furthermore, the complexities of secure aggregation in FL are addressed in Light-
SecAgg, which introduces a lightweight secure aggregation protocol for federated
learning, optimizing privacy and dropout resilience [6]. Using MDS codes, pseudo-
random generators, and parallelization, it enhances efficiency while maintaining
security. However, challenges remain in implementation complexity, quantization
noise, and limited experimental scope. Research gaps include optimizing MDS code
selection, extending evaluations to diverse tasks, and assessing long-term training
impact. Future work should refine security-resilience trade-offs and explore broader
applications beyond image classification.
The broader field of lightweight Federated Learning is explored in a recent research
on lightweight federated learning (TinyFL) that explores frameworks like Tensor-
Flow Federated, PySyft, and FedML for secure, decentralized model training in IoT
environments [23]. Techniques such as model pruning, quantization, and knowledge


                                          7
distillation improve efficiency but face challenges like device heterogeneity, energy
consumption, and security risks. Privacy techniques add complexity, creating gaps in
secure aggregation and adaptive learning. Future research should focus on optimiz-
ing communication, improving energy efficiency, and enhancing security measures
to ensure scalable, practical FL deployment in dynamic IoT systems.
Together, these studies underscore the transformative potential of blockchain in
fortifying ML systems against security vulnerabilities while optimizing scalability
and efficiency. By combining decentralized architectures with advanced consensus
mechanisms, blockchain and ML form a robust foundation for secure and scalable AI-
driven ecosystems. The continued evolution of these frameworks will play a crucial
role in shaping the future of decentralized intelligence across diverse applications,
from healthcare and finance to IoT and beyond.

with scalable performance although operational efficiency remains a concern.

4.1      Design Process or Methodology Overview
4.1.1 Federated Learning: Decentralized Model Training with
Privacy Preservation
Federated Learning (FL) represents a paradigm shift in distributed intelligence—one
that is particularly suited to the financial sector, where regulatory compliance and
confidentiality are paramount [13]. In traditional financial analytics, models are
trained using centrally stored customer data, which raises severe privacy and legal
concerns under frameworks such as GDPR, PCI-DSS, and the Bangladesh Bank
Data Governance Policy[4]. FL addresses this by allowing institutions—such as
banks, credit unions, and regulatory agencies—to collaboratively train a shared
fraud-detection model without sharing their customers’ raw transaction data[13].
Each financial entity operates as an independent node, locally training an instance
of the Adaptive Deep Temporal Context Network (ADTCN) on its internal transac-
tion dataset. After training, the node transmits only model parameters (weights and
gradients) to the blockchain for global aggregation. [5] This process ensures that sen-
sitive details—account numbers, transaction histories, or customer metadata—never
leave institutional boundaries, thus maintaining compliance and protecting compet-
itive privacy.[9]

Applications and usefulness:
   • Inter-bank fraud analytics: Federated ADTCN models can detect coordinated
     fraud patterns across banks without exposing proprietary data.[1]
   • Credit scoring collaboration: Multiple financial institutions can build a com-
     mon model for creditworthiness assessment while preserving customer confi-
     dentiality.[1]
   • Cross-border compliance: Enables secure model sharing between different ju-
     risdictions under privacy laws.[4]

Federated Learning workflow within the system:
  1. Each validator node (bank or financial regulator) trains a local ADTCN model

(DB-BOA): Origin, Design, and Functional Role
Introduction. The Dynamic Butterfly–Billiards Optimization Algorithm (DB-
BOA) forms the optimization backbone of the proposed blockchain–learning ar-
chitecture. It operates as the adaptive intelligence that continuously tunes the
blockchain’s consensus parameters and the deep-learning model’s hyperparameters
to maintain both computational efficiency and predictive accuracy. Unlike tradi-
tional optimization methods with fixed mathematical gradients, DB-BOA is a hybrid
meta-heuristic algorithm — it learns through simulation and stochastic adaptation,
not calculus. Its design philosophy combines the broad exploration capacity of bio-
logical swarm intelligence with the fine precision of physical motion dynamics. The
outcome is an optimizer capable of balancing speed, stability, and scalability within
a decentralized network.
Evolution and Theoretical Lineage. DB-BOA evolved through the fusion of
two independent optimization paradigms:

   • Dynamic Butterfly Optimization Algorithm (DBOA) — inspired by the olfac-
     tory behavior of butterflies. Each candidate solution acts like a butterfly that
     senses “fragrance intensity” proportional to fitness, guiding it toward better
     regions of the search space.
     Strengths: excellent at global exploration and avoiding local minima.
     Weaknesses: higher computational time and slower convergence on large
     datasets.

   • Billiards Optimization Algorithm (BOA) — derived from the physics of elastic
     collisions among billiard balls. Each ball represents a solution, and pockets
     represent optimal targets.
     Strengths: extremely effective for local exploitation and quick convergence.
     Weaknesses: prone to premature convergence when diversity in solutions is
     lost.

The hybrid DB-BOA unites both ideas to exploit their strengths and overcome their
individual shortcomings. It alternates between DBOA-driven exploration and BOA-
driven refinement during each iteration. This dual mechanism allows the optimizer
to search globally during early iterations and converge rapidly as it approaches the
optimal solution.

Unlike centralized deep learning, FL-ADTCN employs the Federated Learning (FL)

and only shares model parameters (not data) with a secure aggregator. This design
satisfies the privacy and compliance constraints inherent in decentralized blockchain

with DB–BOA, FL–ADTCN achieves adaptive global aggregation and hyperparam-

Federated Learning Modes
FL–ADTCN was evaluated under three collaborative learning modes to test its
generalization and privacy-preserving capability:

(a) Centralized Training (Baseline). All training data are aggregated into a
single global server. Advantages: maximum accuracy and fast convergence. Disad-
vantages: high privacy risk and single-point failure.

(b) Federated IID Training. Each client holds Independent and Identically
Distributed (IID) subsets of the data—e.g., every node gets a balanced mixture
of legitimate and fraudulent transactions. Ensures stable training and convergence
close to centralized models, but simulates idealized conditions rarely achievable in
practice.

Identically Distributed (non-IID) data—reflecting real heterogeneity (e.g., some
banks see mostly genuine transactions, others see mostly fraud). Introduces realistic
imbalance and client drift, often causing slower convergence and metric degradation.

Conclusion

This research seeks to develop a federated learning framework that balances model
accuracy, security, and efficiency while preserving data privacy. By integrating
blockchain with optimized consensus mechanisms, privacy-preserving incentives, and
adversarial defense strategies, this study aims to enhance the practicality and secu-

improving scalability, and exploring quantum-resistant cryptographic techniques to
further strengthen security in decentralized ML environments.
