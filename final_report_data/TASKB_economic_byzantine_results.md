**Reading the result (non-obvious — it inverts the usual BFT intuition).** The economic mechanism is strongest exactly where vote-based BFT provably fails: against a *coordinated majority*. With 2 of 3 orgs attacking, a naive equal-weight consensus is dragged to 11.3%–50.0% balanced accuracy, whereas the Shapley→reputation loop drives the attackers to the 0.5 reputation floor by round 3 and, by dropping them from the quorum, recovers 50.0%–90.8% — a +0.0 to +79.5 pp swing. This is because Shapley scores each org against a *trusted validation set* (ground truth), not by counting votes, so a majority cannot out-vote the contribution signal (the trusted-aggregator assumption, see federation_manager docstring & Hsieh et al. 2020).

**The lone-attacker regime, as measured on this dataset.** **always-fraud** — **never isolated**, consensus gap +0.00 pp; **label-flip** — **never isolated**, consensus gap +0.00 pp; **free-rider** — **never isolated**, consensus gap +0.00 pp.

Isolation fired on **0 of 3** single-attacker scenarios, but improved consensus accuracy in **0 of 3**. *Firing and helping are different things, and this table separates them* — a defence that isolates an attacker and then scores worse has not protected anything.

> ⚠ **Do not read a negative single-attacker gap as "isolation is harmful" without accounting for the quorum arithmetic.** Consensus here is `preds.sum() * 2 > n_voters`. With all three orgs voting that is a majority rule (2 of 3); isolating exactly one leaves two voters, where the same expression demands **unanimity**. That shift suppresses positive predictions on its own, independently of whether the isolation decision was correct, and it depresses balanced accuracy on a rare-positive task. The even-voter case is a property of the voting rule, not evidence about the defence.

The mechanism therefore complements, rather than replaces, statistical BFT: it addresses coordinated manipulation, which Krum at f=0 cannot. Whether it deters a lone free-rider is a question this table answers per dataset — not a settled result.
